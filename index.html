Abstract

A reproducible pipeline, and a test of what survives correction

Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) triggers a rapid systemic immune response that is reflected in the peripheral blood transcriptome within hours of onset. Reproducible reanalysis of public RNA-sequencing (RNA-Seq) datasets is essential to consolidate findings, yet many available workflows depend on high-performance computing infrastructure.

We present a fully reproducible bulk RNA-Seq pipeline that runs end-to-end on a commodity workstation and apply it to the NCBI GEO dataset GSE122709, comprising 15 peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) samples: 5 healthy controls (CTRL), 5 stroke samples at 24 h (T24h), and 5 at 7 d (D7) post-onset. The pipeline integrates SRA Toolkit retrieval, FastQC/MultiQC quality control, splice-aware alignment to GRCh38 with HISAT2, gene-level counting with featureCounts against GENCODE v44, differential-expression analysis using DESeq2 with apeglm shrinkage, and functional enrichment using GO, KEGG, and GSEA.

We achieved a mean alignment rate of 95.1% and, under default normalization, identified 9,219 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) at 24 h versus controls, 92 at 7 d versus controls, and 5,851 for T24h versus D7. Principal-component analysis colored by QC metrics revealed a strong correlation between PC1 and the FastQC duplication rate (r = −0.905, p < 10−4). Housekeeping-based RUVg correction reduced the T24h-versus-CTRL DEG count to 28, a 99.7% reduction, whereas the 7 d-versus-control signature remained comparatively robust, decreasing from 92 to 63 DEGs (32% reduction). A 10-gene granulocyte-marker score showed no significant compositional shift (Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.31), and 3-of-5 subsampling confirmed that the 7 d signature is highly sensitive to sample size.

Background · Figure 1

Why look at blood after a stroke?

Ischemic stroke is caused by a clot that blocks a cerebral artery. Within hours, peripheral leukocytes are mobilised, travel to the injured brain and contribute to both damage and repair. Brain tissue is inaccessible in living patients, so PBMCs serve as a clinically tractable view of the post-stroke immune state.

2ndleading cause of mortality worldwide
~85%of all strokes are ischemic
THE GAP

Bulk RNA-Seq gives an unbiased, genome-wide view of these changes, but reproducible whole-genome analysis has historically required HPC resources. When case and control libraries differ systematically in complexity (e.g. duplication rate), the default DESeq2 median-of-ratios normalization can bias DEG estimates unless unwanted-variation correction is applied.

To our knowledge, no prior work has published a complete commodity-workstation pipeline for the GSE122709 PBMC dataset with an integrated sensitivity-to-unwanted-variation assessment.

Pipeline · Figure 2 · Algorithm 1

Seven stages, with unwanted-variation correction before testing

The pipeline follows the canonical bulk RNA-Seq workflow but interleaves a housekeeping-based RUVg stage between quantification and differential-expression testing. Select a stage for details.

DIFFERENTIAL EXPRESSION MODEL

Read count Kij for gene i in sample j is negative binomial, with size factor sj from median-of-ratios and a log-linear model of condition:

Kij ~ NB(μij, αi),   μij = sj qij,   log2 qij = xj⊤βi

Wald test, apeglm log-fold-change shrinkage, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR. A gene is a DEG when padj < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 1. GSEA ranks genes by

ri = sign(log2FCi) · (−log10 pi)

Algorithm 1 · End-to-end RNA-Seq pipeline for stroke PBMC
Require samples S = {s1..s15}, GRCh38 R, GENCODE v44 G, housekeeping set H
Stage 1  for s in S: prefetch(s); fasterq-dump(s, --split-3)
Stage 2  for s in S: FastQC(s);  MultiQC()
Stage 3  for s in S: B_s ← HISAT2(s, R, --dta -p 4)
                      B_s ← SAMtools-sort(B_s, -m 512M -@ 2)
Stage 4  C  ← featureCounts({B_s}, G, -s 0 -p -B -C)
          C' ← genes with ≥10 counts in ≥5 samples
Stage 5  M  ← log2(CPM(C'_H) + 1)        # 21 housekeeping genes
          W  ← SVD_k=2(M − mean(M))       # W = [W1, W2]
Stage 6  for κ in {T24h:CTRL, D7:CTRL, T24h:D7}:
            fit NB GLM ~ condition         # default
            fit NB GLM ~ W1 + W2 + condition # RUVg
            Wald; apeglm; BH-FDR
            D_κ ← {g : padj < 0.05 ∧ |LFC| > 1}
Stage 7  for κ: ORA(D_κ, KEGG, GO-BP); GSEA(rank_κ)
return D, E

24 H · KEY GENES

The strongest down-regulated gene at 24 h was PPBP/CXCL7 (log2FC = −4.69), consistent with platelet consumption at the cerebral thrombus. Notable up-regulated genes included ELANE (neutrophil elastase), S100A12 (alarmin) and the interferon-induced chemokines CXCL9/CXCL10.

7 D · KEY GENES

Of the 92 D7 DEGs, 82 (89%) were also significant at 24 h. The most striking D7 finding was up-regulation of the dual-specificity phosphatases DUSP2 and DUSP8, which dephosphorylate ERK and p38 MAP-kinases.

Discussion

What this shows, and what it does not

ROBUST

Subacute signature and platelet signal

The 7 d signature keeps 63 of 92 DEGs under RUVg, and the PPBP/CXCL7 depression, a strong single-gene monotonic effect, survives as well.

CONDITIONAL

The acute 24 h call set

PC1 tracks duplication (r = −0.905) and RUVg removes 99.7% of the T24h DEGs. The acute DGE list should be treated as a hypothesis conditional on unwanted-variation modelling.

RECOMMENDATION

Report both normalizations

Investigators reanalysing cross-cohort blood transcriptomic datasets should routinely report QC-coloured PCA and DEG counts under both default and RUVg-adjusted normalization.

LIMITATIONS
  • n = 5 per group from a single cohort: hypothesis-generating only
  • Cannot establish brain-tissue neutrophil activity, cell-type mechanisms or prognostic panels; no clinical outcomes analysed
  • Read pairs assigned to genes (34–47%) below the ~60% benchmark
  • Only two post-stroke timepoints
FUTURE WORK
  • Longitudinal sampling of the same patients
  • Single-cell RNA-Seq to resolve cell-type programs
  • Outcome-annotated cohorts with matched library-preparation protocols
